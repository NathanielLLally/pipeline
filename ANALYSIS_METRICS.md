# Draft Analysis Metrics & Rubric

## Overview

The draft analysis agent evaluates outreach emails across **8 core quality dimensions**, each scored 0-10, plus structured feedback on strengths and improvement areas.

All scores use a **0-10 scale** where:
- **0-3**: Significant issues; likely to underperform
- **4-6**: Decent but with clear gaps; room for improvement
- **7-8**: Strong; likely to perform well
- **9-10**: Exceptional; likely to generate strong response

---

## Scoring Dimensions

### 1. **Personalization Score** (0-10)

How specifically tailored is this email to **THIS specific business**?

**Scoring Rubric:**
- **0-2**: Completely generic; could send to any business type
  - Example: "Dear business owner, we have great leads..."
- **3-4**: Mentions the business name but otherwise generic
  - No reference to specific business model or context
- **5-6**: References some specific business detail
  - Mentions industry or shows surface-level research
- **7-8**: Clear specific knowledge of this business
  - References specific services, pain signals, or business model details
- **9-10**: Obviously unique to this specific business
  - Shows deep understanding of their operation, tone, and specific situation

**What Matters:**
- Specific business name (not just "you")
- Reference to specific services they offer
- Knowledge of their business model/customer base
- Specific pain signals they face
- Demonstrated research into their particular operation

---

### 2. **Pain Points Score** (0-10)

How well does the email address **their specific pain signals**?

**Scoring Rubric:**
- **0-2**: Completely misses the mark
  - Mentions wrong pain signals
  - Proposes solution for a problem they don't have
- **3-4**: Vague reference to generic pain
  - "Businesses need customers" (too generic)
- **5-6**: Addresses some identified pain points
  - Gets some right but misses others or doesn't articulate them clearly
- **7-8**: Clearly articulates their specific pain signals
  - References multiple relevant pain points
- **9-10**: Perfect articulation of their exact problem
  - Makes recipient say "yes, exactly this is our issue"

**What Matters:**
- Specific language around their actual pain signals
- Multiple pain points addressed (not just one)
- Accuracy to their situation
- Making them feel *understood* and *seen*

---

### 3. **Call-to-Action Hook Score** (0-10)

How compelling is the hook? Does it create urgency or desire to respond?

**Scoring Rubric:**
- **0-2**: No hook; no reason to respond
  - Just presents information
  - No urgency, appeal, or curiosity
- **3-4**: Weak hook
  - Vague appeal ("could help you")
  - No specific reason to act
- **5-6**: Present but not compelling
  - Creates mild interest
  - Recipient might respond but isn't compelled to
- **7-8**: Strong hook
  - Creates real appeal or curiosity
  - Makes recipient want to learn more
- **9-10**: Highly compelling
  - Recipient feels compelled to respond immediately
  - Clear, specific reason to engage
  - Taps into real motivation/desire

**What Matters:**
- Specificity of the appeal
- Urgency or scarcity signals (if appropriate)
- Curiosity gap (makes them want to know more)
- Relevance to their goals/pain
- Fear of missing out (FOMO) or gain/benefit focus
- Clear "next step" action

---

### 4. **Business Relevance Score** (0-10)

How well does the offer fit **this business model**?

**Scoring Rubric:**
- **0-2**: Irrelevant to their business
  - Wrong business model
  - Solution doesn't match their revenue stream
- **3-4**: Tangentially relevant
  - Could theoretically help but unclear how
- **5-6**: Relevant but not perfect fit
  - Makes sense for their business but may not be core need
- **7-8**: Natural fit for their business
  - Clear alignment with their customer acquisition strategy
- **9-10**: Perfect fit for their business model
  - The offer is an obvious extension of their business

**What Matters:**
- Understanding their revenue stream
- Knowing their customer acquisition challenges
- Proposing something that fits their budget/ROI expectations
- Offering something they would actually want to pay for
- Alignment with their business growth strategy

---

### 5. **Authenticity Score** (0-10)

Does this sound like a real person or a form letter?

**Scoring Rubric:**
- **0-2**: Obviously templated
  - Robotic language, no personality
  - Generic phrases throughout
  - Clearly a form letter
- **3-4**: Slightly templated
  - Some specific details added to otherwise generic email
  - Robotic tone throughout
- **5-6**: Somewhat authentic
  - Mix of genuine and templated language
  - Some personality but inconsistent
- **7-8**: Authentic voice
  - Reads like a real person wrote it
  - Conversational tone, genuine interest
- **9-10**: Highly authentic
  - Distinctive voice and perspective
  - Feels genuinely interested in this specific business
  - Human, conversational, warm

**What Matters:**
- Conversational (not corporate) language
- Personal pronouns and perspective ("I noticed...")
- Specific, concrete details (not abstractions)
- Shorter sentences and natural rhythm
- Lack of marketing jargon
- Evidence of real thought/effort

---

### 6. **Clarity Score** (0-10)

Is the message crystal clear and easy to understand?

**Scoring Rubric:**
- **0-2**: Confusing; hard to parse
  - Unclear what's being offered
  - Complex jargon without explanation
- **3-4**: Somewhat unclear
  - Main idea present but requires re-reading
  - Some confusing language or structure
- **5-6**: Reasonably clear
  - Understand the offer with one reading
  - Minor unclear sections
- **7-8**: Very clear
  - Immediately understand what's being offered and why
- **9-10**: Crystal clear
  - Anyone reading once knows exactly what's being offered and why

**What Matters:**
- Simple language (avoid jargon)
- Clear structure and flow
- One main idea per sentence/paragraph
- Specific, concrete language (not abstract)
- Short subject line and preview text
- Body length (not too long)

---

### 7. **Trust Signals Score** (0-10)

Does it include credibility elements that build trust?

**Scoring Rubric:**
- **0-2**: No trust elements
  - No proof, credentials, or social proof
- **3-4**: Minimal trust signals
  - Maybe mentions experience but vague
- **5-6**: Some trust signals
  - References past success or credentials
- **7-8**: Clear trust-building elements
  - Specific examples, results, or proof
  - References to similar businesses helped
- **9-10**: Strong credibility signals
  - Specific, quantified results
  - References to similar successful businesses
  - Clear proof of competence

**What Matters:**
- Specific track record or results (with numbers if possible)
- Reference to similar businesses you've worked with
- Credentials or experience level
- Social proof or testimonials (if appropriate)
- Track record in their specific industry
- Time in business / experience level

---

### 8. **Overall Quality Score** (0-10)

Holistic assessment: **What's the likelihood this email gets a positive response?**

**Scoring Rubric:**
- **0-2**: Likely to be deleted immediately
  - Combines multiple issues
  - Will harm your sender reputation
- **3-4**: Low response probability
  - Too many issues to be effective
- **5-6**: Moderate response probability
  - Decent but nothing special
  - Average outreach quality
- **7-8**: High response probability
  - Strong email that will likely get responses
  - Multiple strengths, few weaknesses
- **9-10**: Very high response probability
  - Excellent email; likely to generate strong interest
  - All elements working together effectively

**Synthesis:**
This score synthesizes all other dimensions. A strong overall score means:
- Personalization + authenticity + clarity = recipient feels *understood*
- Pain points + CTA hook = recipient feels *motivated to respond*
- Business relevance + trust signals = recipient feels *confident*

---

## Key Strengths & Improvement Areas

For each draft, the analysis identifies:

- **Key Strengths**: Top 2-3 things the draft does well
  - Use these as a baseline for future drafts
  - Build on what works

- **Improvement Areas**: Top 2-3 things that could be improved
  - Most impactful changes to increase response rate
  - Quick wins vs. deep structural changes

## Integration with run_agents.py

When integrated into `run_agents.py`, the analysis flow:

1. Runs **after drafting succeeds** for each business
2. Includes research context and business data
3. Produces structured metrics for each draft
4. Stores results in Prefect variables (searchable, queryable)
5. Includes analysis in the flow outcome
6. Never blocks drafting (analysis errors are logged, not fatal)

### Output Structure

Each business outcome includes:

```python
{
    'business_id': '...',
    'business_name': '...',
    'research_status': 'researched',
    'draft_status': 'drafted',
    'draft': {...},
    'analysis_status': 'analyzed',
    'analysis': {
        'personalization_score': 7.5,
        'personalization_rationale': '...',
        'pain_points_score': 8.0,
        'pain_points_rationale': '...',
        # ... all 8 scores with rationales
        'key_strengths': [...],
        'improvement_areas': [...],
    }
}
```

## Using analyze_drafts.py Standalone

For batch analysis of existing drafts:

```python
from flow.analyze_drafts import analyze_batch_drafts

results = await analyze_batch_drafts([
    {
        'business': {'id': '1', 'business_name': 'Acme Dog Training', ...},
        'research': {'personalization_hook': '...', ...},
        'draft': {'subject': '...', 'body': '...'},
    },
    # ... more drafts
])
```

Returns aggregate statistics:

```python
{
    'stats': {
        'total': 10,
        'analyzed': 9,
        'errored': 1,
    },
    'score_aggregates': {
        'personalization_score': {'mean': 7.2, 'min': 5, 'max': 9, 'count': 9},
        'pain_points_score': {'mean': 7.5, 'min': 6, 'max': 9, 'count': 9},
        # ... all scores
    },
    'results': [...]  # detailed results if output_format='detailed'
}
```

## Interpretation Guide

### Score Distribution

**Good benchmarks for each dimension:**

- **Personalization**: 7+ indicates good personalization
- **Pain Points**: 7+ indicates strong pain articulation
- **CTA Hook**: 6+ indicates motivating hook; 8+ very compelling
- **Business Relevance**: 7+ indicates good fit
- **Authenticity**: 7+ indicates genuine voice
- **Clarity**: 8+ indicates very clear message
- **Trust Signals**: 6+ indicates some credibility; 8+ strong proof
- **Overall Quality**: 7+ likely generates responses; 8+ strong response rate

### Using Scores for Improvement

1. **Identify lowest scoring dimension** across all drafts
   - Focus improvement efforts there
   - Example: If clarity is consistently 5-6, simplify language

2. **Identify high performers**
   - What makes them score 8+?
   - What can you replicate?

3. **Track trends over time**
   - Monitor if scores improve with template changes
   - Benchmark against baseline

4. **A/B testing**
   - Analyze different draft approaches
   - Compare scores to predict winner

## Technical Details

- **Scoring**: All scores are continuous (0.0-10.0)
- **Rationales**: Each score includes 1-2 sentence explanation with specific examples
- **Model**: Uses configured `LLM_MODEL` for analysis (same as research/drafting)
- **Schema**: Validates against `DraftAnalysisOutput` Pydantic model
- **Artifacts**: Input/output saved to flow run working directory
- **Variables**: Results stored in Prefect variables for querying
